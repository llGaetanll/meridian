Parts

The app's language, drawn at the size it is drawn on the phone, and live — tap anything here. Every screen below is assembled out of these, and the same set is what the web and the desktop app should be speaking, so this sheet wants a page of its own before long.

Chip
An outer hairline, three pixels of ground, and the fill inset inside it. Selected is the accent tint with the label in violet-300 and the border warmed by the same amount — no glow: on a row of nine of them it was nine little lamps.
Chip row
Where the order of a row of chips carries no meaning, it is chosen to fill the measure — and chosen properly. The script costs every way of cutting the chips into lines by the square of what each line wastes and takes the cheapest; then the chips on a line share out what is left, so the row is flush at both edges. Greedy packing got close and then left one line short, which is the ragged edge you can see from across the room.

Range
One outline with four options inside it and the live one filled — the 1D/1W/1M selector stood on its end. Four separately outlined rows was four controls sitting near each other; this is one control with four settings, which is what it is, and every row is the same height because in an enum no case is bigger than another. Between stays drawn and dead: picking a day needs a date picker, and that is a screen of its own.
Button
All of them raised, on a solid line of their own darker shade, and all of them drop onto it when pressed. The quiet one had to come up a step to manage it: it was sitting at bg-input on an edge of --bg, which is the page, so its edge was a black line drawn on black. The violet one carries a two-step gradient of the same violet — enough to catch a highlight across the top, not enough to read as a gradient. Its light falls downward rather than haloing: a blurred shadow at no offset rounds the corners off, which is why the last one looked like a lozenge and dimmer at the corners than along the edges.
Button group
The same buttons with the gap taken out: they share their side borders and their edges run into one line under the row. The live one is the violet button exactly — same gradient, same edge, same light — so a group is a row of buttons with one of them pressed rather than a control of its own. Nothing uses it yet.
Plain button
No frame and no edge, so it presses down a pixel of its own — violet text and an icon. For an action that has to be available without being the loudest thing on the screen, which is exactly Reconnect sitting in a row of bank names. The icon is “open in new”, and it is telling the truth: Plaid Link in update mode is a hosted page, so the tap leaves the app.

Rules
Two weights, and both run the full width of the screen. Strong between the parts of a page; faint between rows and between groups of rows — which is what the ledger was missing, being one undivided column while every other page had a shape to scroll by.

Calendar day
Plain, marked, today, tapped, outside the month. Today is the accent as a wash; a tapped day is filled in grey rather than outlined. Marks say something landed and no longer say what — the split above the calendar says that, in words.
Palette
Violet is you — what you picked, where you are, what day it is. Green and rose are money in and money owed, and only ever on a figure. The other three are text.

Ledger

The range is one framed control with the live option filled inside it, every row the same height. The band the filter leaves behind has no ground of its own — a rule under it is what separates it, a chevron says it opens, and what is in it is the app's own chips. And the figures make one column: a price sits with its merchant, at the size of the day's net, on the same right edge.

Chips fill the line, properly
Every partition costed and the cheapest taken, then the chips on each line share out the remainder so the row is flush both sides. Greedy got close and left one line short, which is the ragged edge you could see from across the room.
The range is one control
One outline, four options, the live one filled — the 1D/1W/1M selector on its end. Every row the same height, because in an enum no case is bigger than another.
The band is separated by a rule
No ground of its own after all — a rule under it does the work, like every other boundary in the app. A chevron on the left says it opens, and the two halves inside it are the app's chips at a smaller size rather than pills invented for the band.
One column of figures
A price sits on the merchant's baseline, at the size of the day's net, and on the same right edge — the rows carried 4px of inset the day heading did not, which is exactly far enough out of line to see and not far enough to explain.
A rule between days
Each day is a group now, with the faint rule above its heading — and the heading already carries the day's net, so the two halves of the line say the same thing. The first day of a month goes without: the month rule is right above it.

The share stands on the rule
Twice as tall, resting on the line rather than centred across it, and starting at the measure the words are set to rather than at the edge of the glass — so a share is a share of the line you are reading. A little light under each one.
No week that is all next month
July's sixth row was seven August days and nothing else. Worth knowing: this makes the grid's height depend on the month, and pinning it at six rows was how the height was kept still while you swipe. If it jumps annoyingly, that is the trade.
The calendar runs sideways
The month gives up a little of the measure so the columns either side have somewhere to be, and they sit further from it than the days inside it sit from each other — they are the next page, not the next column. Both fade out at the glass.
No signs on the diff
“$4.22 in”, “$3,771.40 out”. The words carry the direction, so the plus and minus were saying it twice; the words go grey and only the figures are money.
Only the edges are drawn
The neighbouring months are a single column each rather than whole grids, clipped to the row count of the month they flank so they never hang below it. Enough to see, not enough to swipe to: building that means three grids and a snap, which is worth doing in the app and not worth 84 more cells in a sketch.
Still: this turns the feed around
The page reads Plaid's predicted next dates today. Looking backwards means reading each stream's history instead — a real change in what the core asks for, not a redraw.

Balances

Room to breathe: the held section has a floor under it now, blocks sit 30px apart, and every rule has the same air either side. Block totals stay grey, so the account rows are the numbers you read and the section heading is the only place on the page money takes a colour. Drawn with Citi, Fidelity and Amex — already filed as issues #11, #13 and #12 — and the figures are the only invented data in this file.

Block totals go grey
A block total is scaffolding, not a figure to read — the account rows above it are the numbers. That takes six competing figures off the screen and leaves the section heading as the only coloured money on the page.
Room between the sections
The held section had no floor under it, so its last total ended flush against the rule that starts Owed — a boundary with air on one side of it only. 30px under the section, 26 above the heading, and every block's rule with the same air either side.
Reconnect loses its frame
A solid violet block in the middle of a list of bank names was the loudest thing on a page that has nothing urgent on it. Violet text, an “open in new” icon, no frame. The icon is honest: Plaid Link in update mode is a hosted page, so the tap leaves the app.
Is it quiet enough yet
The remaining density is one line of type under every account name — mask, APY, availability. That is the next thing I would cut if it still reads as eclectic, and it is a real loss, so say the word rather than my guessing.
