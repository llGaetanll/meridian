Web, proposed

Ledger, Recurring and Balances, at the width a laptop has. The controls are sketches/design-language.html and not one of them is restyled here; what changes between the phone and this is only ever what the extra width is spent on.

It is spent on two things. A line the phone has to assemble into one sentence gets columns — a transaction's category and account, an account's type and what is available on it — because columns are what let you compare two rows without reading either to the end. And the one control that decides what a page says gets a column of its own: the ledger's filter, the recurring calendar, the split of what you hold across the banks that hold it. The rail scrolls with the page, not against it — one scroller on the screen, and the sidebar is the only thing that holds still.

That second one is what kills three phone controls outright. The filter band, the sideways calendar and the swipe that turns it all exist because a phone has one column of room, and none of them should be ported.

The shell

A sidebar that says where you are, one scroller beside it, and half the nav drawn and dimmed because the markets side is real work we have not done. Below, the same shell three times.

The page title
The nav says where you are, lit in violet, so no page on the web opens on its own name. The phone needs its 30px title because its dock is three icons at the bottom of the screen.
Half the nav is drawn and dimmed
Home, Portfolios and Markets are real work that is coming, and sketches/desktop/ already draws them. Dimming them is the chip's own drawn-but-not-built state, and it is more honest than a nav that pretends the app is three pages and then has to grow.
Spending and Connections
What the Spending sketch drew is what the Ledger's filter and Recurring's split now do. Connections live on Balances, next to the balances they carry — a page listing banks and saying nothing about their money is a settings screen.
Search is drawn and dead
The same state the dock's search button is in on the phone: the sketches have never drawn the screen behind it.
One scroller
The sidebar stays put and everything right of it moves together — the rail included.

Ledger

The filter gets a column of its own, which takes the band with it and puts a hero at the top of the list: a filter you can read at a glance is a filter the list owes a figure to. The row spreads into columns, and the day heading is the one thing on the page that pins.

The band
On the phone the filter scrolls away and comes back as a summary strip you tap to get back to it. Here it has a column of its own, so what it is set to is never something the list has to say on its behalf. The band is a phone control and should stay one.
Clear
Every chip and every range option already says whether it is on. A button whose only job is to put them all back is one more thing on a page that has no other button — and the range's own “All time” is the clear.
A hero on the Ledger
With the filter always on screen, the top of the list has to say what the filter comes to — otherwise the answer to “what did I spend on food this year” is a list you have to add up. ViewLedger needs the four fields ViewRecurring already has: a label, a figure, and the two flows.
Two more columns
Category and account each get a column instead of being a subtitle under the merchant, so the eye can run down one of them without reading the others. The account is the new field on ViewTxRow; the category is already there.
The filter goes by too
One scroller means that three months down the list the rail is a screen behind you, which is the thing the phone's band is for. If a band ever comes back here it belongs at the top of the rail's column and not over the list — but the honest first answer is that a filter you set once and read down from may not need one.
The day heading still pins
The same position: sticky the phone uses, and the only thing on the page that moves while you scroll. The rule belongs to the row above it, so a pinned heading does not drag a second line up with it.
A bug the narrow rail found
The chip packer sorted its lines by the widest chip on each, meaning to put the fullest line at the top. The cheapest partition puts the widest chip on the emptiest line, so at 300px it hoisted one lonely chip above four full lines. It sorts by line span now — shared/controls.js, and the phone's two copies want the same fix.
Nine chips, or sixteen?
ViewFilter sends all sixteen categories with their live counts. Nine fill five lines of a 300px rail; sixteen would fill nine. Either the rail is wider than the sidebar, or the empty ones stay collapsed until you ask.
Two categories, and scrolled — the day heading pinned

The swipe, and the columns either side
Months run sideways on the phone because one month is all that fits, and the half-column peeking at each edge is what says so. A whole month fits the rail, so the month sits centred with an arrow on each side and nothing is cut off at the glass. The arrows are the plain button: framed, they would be two controls flanking a label rather than one line you step along.
One scroller, not two
The rail moves with the page rather than holding still in it. A rail pinned under the same pointer as the page is two scroll areas and no way to tell which one a wheel will turn — we have had that fight already.
The stream row gets its parts back
frequency, when and soon are already three fields on ViewStreamRow; the phone joins them into sub because it has one line. The web prints all three and needs nothing new.
Transfer in is in the list, not in the split
“Where it goes” is where it goes. The one stream that brings money in is in the list below, in its own group, where it does not have to be a negative slice of a positive bar.
The marks are still mute
A dot says a stream lands, never which. That is right on the phone, where the split is a screenful above. Here the split is beside it — worth asking whether hovering a day should light its row in the split.
Scrolled — every stream, grouped the way the split is

The one violet button
Adding a bank is the thing you come to this page to do, and Balances is the only one of the three with such a thing. Drawn dead until Plaid Link is wired, the same as Reconnect.
The subtitle wants to be columns
ViewAccountRow.subtitle is one assembled sentence — “Checking ·4021 · $4,080.22 available” — which is right for one narrow line and wrong for a table. Two finished fields instead of one, kind and detail, and the phone joins them the way it already does.
Where it sits
The blocks down the page say what each bank holds. The rail says how much of your money each one is, which is the question a page of six accounts across four banks actually raises. It is the recurring split, reused.
Block totals stay grey
Same as the phone: the account rows are the numbers, the block total is scaffolding, and the section heading is the only place on the page money takes a colour.
Citi is in both halves
The current account is money you have and the card is money you owe, and they are one connection. Two blocks, one bank — and the rail says it twice too, for the same reason.
